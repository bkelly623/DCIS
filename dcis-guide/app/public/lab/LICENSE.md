# Experimental artwork and technology

Pip is an original procedurally authored fictional crystal companion. SVG artwork in src/Lab.tsx and GLB geometry generated by scripts/generate-lab-assets.mjs were created for this DCIS prototype; no copied mascot, scanned museum specimen, external character model, texture or proprietary artwork is used. These new Pip SVG/GLB assets and the new tabletop geometry are offered under CC0-1.0. This does not relicense museum photographs or underlying museum source materials.

The tabletop uses the existing public app's schematic Mineral Hall outline and three public cabinet anchors. Cabinet heights, decorative crystals and proportions are illustrative, not surveyed. Real-object photos and interpretation are reused unchanged from the existing curated public app selection, with their existing rights and limitations.

Dependencies bundled locally through npm/Vite:
- @google/model-viewer: Apache-2.0, https://github.com/google/model-viewer
- three: MIT, https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js
License texts remain in the installed npm packages; source distributions preserve their notices.

Model-viewer primary documentation: https://modelviewer.dev/examples/augmentedreality/ and https://modelviewer.dev/docs/ . AR modes: webxr, scene-viewer, quick-look. Omitting ios-src enables model-viewer USDZ generation on Quick Look launch. Native viewers display the chosen static GLB appearance, not React controls/SVG animation. AR requires supported hardware/browser and a secure context; physical AR has not been tested.

Crystal workshop: a deliberately schematic analogy for ordered repetition, not atomic structure, actual growth kinetics, or a reproduction of any museum specimen. Beryl & Microcline linkage and photographs come from existing public record DCIS-WR-000082 in Case 3. General crystal background: https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-difference-between-a-rock-and-a-mineral .
