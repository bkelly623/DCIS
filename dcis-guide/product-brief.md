# Product Brief

## Working Name

DCIS Guide

## Audience

Primary:

- museum visitors
- families
- students
- curious local residents

Secondary:

- DCIS staff
- volunteers
- tour guides
- researchers
- educators

## Visitor Experience

The visitor opens the app, gives the guide a little context, then explores the museum with the phone camera and a conversational guide.

The guide should ask a few light discovery questions before starting:

- How much time do you have?
- Who is visiting?
- What kind of tour sounds good?
- Do you want weird, local, kid-friendly, beautiful, science-heavy, history-heavy, or highlights?

During the tour, the visitor can point the camera at a room, case, label, or object. The guide responds with a short, engaging interpretation and can go deeper if asked.

The visitor can interrupt at any point:

- What am I looking at?
- Why is this important?
- Is this local?
- What's the strangest thing in this case?
- What should I look at next?
- Is there a story behind this?

## Product Personality

The guide should feel proactive, entertaining, and informed. It should not sound like a database row or generic encyclopedia.

Possible guide styles:

- polished docent
- local naturalist
- dramatic storyteller
- kid-friendly explorer
- 19th-century science voice
- field biologist

The same knowledge can be rendered in different styles based on the visitor.

## Game And Engagement Ideas

- discovery quests
- mystery clues
- hidden object trails
- specimen unlocks
- visitor field notes
- seasonal challenges
- family scavenger hunts
- "most visitors miss this" moments
- repeat-visit achievements

The game layer should encourage attention, movement, questions, and repeat visits.

## Success Criteria

- visitors use it for more than a few minutes
- visitors move to additional exhibits because of it
- visitors ask questions
- children and adults both find useful modes
- visitors react, laugh, share, or show someone else
- staff can add knowledge without needing a separate technical workflow
- DCIS gains reusable exhibit knowledge after every internal walkthrough
